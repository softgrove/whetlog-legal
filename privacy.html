Whetlog

Privacy Policy

Effective date: August 29, 2026 · Last updated: September 15, 2026

Local logbook data, limited anonymous usage data. Everything you record — blades, stones, sharpening sessions, notes, and photos — stays on your device and is never sent to us. Whetlog sends limited anonymous usage events to a server operated by the developer. There is no account, advertising, or third-party tracking.

Who we are

Whetlog (“the app”, “we”) is provided by Softgrove, an independent developer based in Japan. Contact: softgrove@duck.com.

What data the app handles

You may record blades, whetstones, sharpening sessions, touch-ups, notes, optional photos, and related details such as dates, grit, duration, and ratings (“your log”). Your log is stored only locally on your device using Apple’s on-device SwiftData database. We never receive this data, and the app does not provide account sync or a cloud backup service.

No log content is sent. This includes blade and stone details, session notes, photos, and ratings.

Anonymous usage analytics

Whetlog sends events for app launches, saving a sharpening session, adding a stone or blade to your collection, Lifetime offer views, purchase-related actions (purchase start, completion, and failure with a fixed reason), restoring a purchase, and reaching an export limit as a non-premium user. Each event contains the app version, a timestamp, the event name, and, when applicable, a paywall trigger or product SKU.

These events do not include any identifier: there is no UUID, account ID, advertising identifier (IDFA), or device identifier (IDFV) generated, stored, or sent. Whetlog does not access IDFA or IDFV. The events cannot be linked back to a specific device or person; the developer can only see aggregate counts.

We use these events only to understand which features are used and where people stop in the app flow so we can improve Whetlog. We do not use them for advertising or tracking.

Each request contains the app identifier (a), app version (v), event name (e), timestamp (t), and a small properties object (p). Current properties contain only fixed internal screen or flow names and, where relevant, an App Store product identifier. They cannot contain user-entered content or free-form text. No device or installation identifier is included.

Cloudflare, Inc. hosts the endpoint as our infrastructure processor. Our Worker does not store source IP addresses, User-Agent headers, raw event bodies, or raw timestamps. It stores daily aggregate counts only for this app. Cloudflare may process network metadata to deliver and secure its service under its data-processing terms.

Export, sharing, and deletion

  • Export / Share: You can export a blade card or CSV file and share it using the system share sheet. Exported files are under your control once you share them; nothing is sent to us. Non-premium accounts are limited on some export actions; reaching that limit is what triggers the export event described above.
  • Delete: You can delete any entry at any time. Deleting the app also removes its on-device data.

Purchases and payments

Whetlog offers an optional Lifetime unlock as a one-time purchase, sold and processed by Apple through the App Store. We do not receive or store your payment information. Your purchase entitlement is verified on-device through Apple’s StoreKit framework.

Network connections

The logbook and sharpening-tracking features work fully offline. When a network is available, Whetlog may send the anonymous usage events described above to a server operated by the developer. Apple’s system frameworks also communicate with the App Store for purchases and purchase restoration.

Children

Whetlog is not directed to children under 13 (or the minimum age in your region). The app does not knowingly collect personal information from children.

Third parties

Whetlog has no third-party analytics or advertising SDKs. We use Cloudflare, Inc. only to host the anonymous event endpoint and aggregated database described above. We do not sell these events, provide them to third parties for their own purposes, use them for advertising, or use them to track you across apps or websites.
